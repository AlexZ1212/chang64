01  /  The direction

Keep the club. Throw out the clubhouse. Felt, brass fittings and boxwood are set dressing — they say old where you wanted to say serious. The thing that actually reads as a real chess club is not the furniture, it is the discipline: notation kept properly, the engine put away during play, nothing decorative on the board.

So the ground moves from green felt to ink — a near‑black with just enough green in it to remember where it came from. The board stops being a picture of a tournament board and becomes the highest‑contrast object on the screen: warm bone against a cool slate‑teal, separated by lightness rather than by hue, so it survives colour blindness by construction. Brass stays, because one warm accent against a cold field is the whole colour idea and it already works. Mono for data stays. The scoresheet stays — ruled rows are not nostalgia, they are how the game is recorded.

One font changes, not three. Fraunces has a wobble in it that reads “craft brand”; Source Serif is flatter, calmer and holds up at 14px on a content page, which is where 1300 of your pages live.

Is “chess club” right, or is it nostalgia?
Right register, wrong century. Aim at a club that was refurbished — good light, hard surfaces, the trophies still on the wall. Nothing distressed, no wood grain, no leather. Restraint is the club signal; ornament is the giveaway.
Elephant — gift or distraction?
A gift, on one condition: it appears once. In the mark, and nowhere else — the bishop only borrows the angle of its trunk for the mitre cut. Never in the UI, never on the board, never as a mascot. Used once it explains the name; used twice it becomes a theme.
Is dark right?
Dark for the app, light for the library. The board is where people look and it is lit either way; evening play wants a dark surround. But a 900‑word page on back‑rank mates arriving cold from Google should be black on bone. Two themes, split by job, not by a toggle. Same twelve tokens, one alternate set.

04  /  The pieces

Turned, not drawn

Six silhouettes with the profile of a lathe-turned set — concave waists, ball finials, a collar ring — carried entirely by the outline, so there is no shading to lose and no fine detail to blur at 40px. Every piece stands on the same two turned elements — a collar ring and a stepped foot — and they are deliberately small: the base takes a fifth of the height, so the mass sits up where the identifying happens. Nothing floats: the pawn’s ball sits on a turned neck under its own ring, and the bishop’s finial is part of the mitre rather than a dot resting on it. Ranked by silhouette weight: pawn (round), rook (square), bishop (ovoid), knight (angular), queen (spiked), king (broad + cross). No two share a family.

Bishop and knight separate on construction: the bishop is curves, vertical and symmetrical; the knight is the only asymmetric silhouette in the set, and it carries an S on its left edge — nose out, throat scooped in, chest out again. That lean, and the three interior marks it alone carries, are what keep the pair apart at 26px.
The elephant, once
The bishop's cut is a real cut: it breaks the mitre's outline on the upper right and ends flat, the way a slit sawn into boxwood ends. It is set at the same angle as the trunk in the mark, and that is as far as the reference should go here — the elephant belongs to the mark, and a piece that has to survive 40px is the wrong place to hide a second one.

05  /  The board and its states

Four channels, so nothing collides

Ten states that stack is a layering problem, not a colour problem. Give each state a channel — a physical layer of the square it is allowed to use — and two states can never fight, because they are not drawing in the same place. Within a channel, one state wins; across channels, they simply coexist.

Channel 1 — wash
Flat tint over the whole square. History and verdict only: last move, puzzle correct, puzzle wrong. Never more than one; latest wins.
Channel 2 — inset edge
A border drawn inside the square. Intent: selected (3px solid), hint and engine suggestion (2px pulsing), keyboard focus (2px dashed, inset 3px).
Channel 3 — centre mark
A shape at the square's centre, under the piece. Legality: dot for a quiet move, ring for a capture. Nothing else may use the centre.
Channel 4 — glow
A radial bleed from the square centre outward. Check only. Reserved permanently, so a king in check is never ambiguous no matter what else is on that square.
default
bone / board, 2px corner on the outer board only

puzzle — wrong
wash brick 50% + 420ms shake, ±4px. Reduced motion: wash only, held 600ms longer.

inset edge — 2px brass, 1.6s pulse. Reduced motion: static 2px at 70%.
keyboard focus
inset edge — 2px dashed chalk, offset in 3px. Chalk, never brass: focus must survive a brass square.
all three at once
selected + last move + check. Wash, glow and edge occupy different layers, so it still parses.

06  /  The logo

A head, a trunk, and a number that behaves like a number

The line for the About page: Chang is Thai for elephant — the piece that became the bishop, still called elephant across half the world. The mark is that animal, front on, on the sixty-four squares.

Wordmark spec

08  /  Things you did not ask about
The threshold screen is a scoresheet header
Play-before-start should look like the top of a blank scoresheet: date, colour, time control, opponent, all filled in, ruled, with the empty move grid below and the frozen position beside it. It stops reading as a broken game the moment it reads as a form waiting to be filled.
Ship an OG image generator, not an OG image
You already render pieces server-side onto 1300 pages. Render the same board into a 1200×630 card per page — position, title, mark. That is 1300 distinct search previews for roughly the cost of one template, and it is the single highest-leverage design asset you have.
Coordinates on, permanently
Not a setting. Mono 500 at 9px in the square corner, coloured as the opposite square. Your audience is people learning to see the board — and the coordinate drill is a mode you already built. Making them optional undercuts your own feature.
Cut the stats strip from five to three
Level, solved, best streak, puzzle rating, day streak — three of those are streaks and it starts to feel like an app that wants you back. Keep level, puzzle rating and solved. Day streak is the single most esports-app element on the site.
The eval bar is bone and ink, never brass
It is the board's colours turned on their side — white's share in bone, black's in the piece ink. Brass means “you did this”; the evaluation is not something you did. Keep the accent scarce or it stops meaning anything.
One motion rule
Pieces move in 130ms ease-out; everything else in the interface is 0ms or 160ms opacity. No slides, no scales, no springs. A chess site where the UI moves more than the pieces has its priorities wrong — and it makes the reduced-motion fallback nearly free.
