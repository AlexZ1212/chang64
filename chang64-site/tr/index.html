The elephant on 64 squares

Chang () is the Thai word for elephant. It is also, by a long detour, a chess piece. The game began in India as chaturanga, whose four divisions were infantry, cavalry, chariots and elephants.

The elephant travelled west and changed its name at every border: gaja in Sanskrit, al-fil in Arabic, alfil in Spanish, and at last the bishop in English and the fou in French. It never left Russian, where the same piece is still slon, elephant, nor Chinese chess, where it is written 象. Thailand kept a game of its own, makruk, played there to this day.

Sixty-four is the rest of the name: the squares. chang64 is the elephant on the sixty-four squares, a piece that crossed a thousand years and half the world, on a board that never changed.

      Our approach

      We follow the criteria of the RGAA, the French accessibility standard. No official audit has been done yet.

      • The board can be played entirely with the keyboard.
      • Moves and game state can be read aloud by your screen reader.
      • A link at the top of the page skips straight to the content.
      • Text contrast is checked in every theme.
      • Animations follow your system setting and can be turned off.
      • Buttons are large enough to be tapped easily.
      • Every image that carries meaning has a text alternative.
